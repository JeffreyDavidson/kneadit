# KneadIt — Design Context

This file is the persistent design context for KneadIt. All design-skill work
(`frontend-design`, `audit`, `polish`, etc.) should read this first before
producing visual decisions.

KneadIt is a multi-tenant SaaS for cottage-food bakers. Each tenant gets their
own customer-facing storefront on a subdomain (`bakery-name.getkneadit.app`).
The same base storefront template ships to every tenant; tenants customize via
brand colors, hero images, page content, and a theme variant
(`modern` / `classic` / `rustic` / `elegant` / `yellow`).

## Design Context

### Users

Two distinct user groups, both serviced by the same storefront:

- **Customers** ordering baked goods online — typically suburban, ages 25–55,
  buying for a household or a small gift occasion (birthday cake, dinner-party
  dessert, weekend treats). Often local to the baker. They land on the site
  via word-of-mouth, Instagram, or a Google search for "bakery near me." The
  job: confirm this is a real, trustworthy bakery that makes things they want,
  then place an order with minimum friction.
- **Bakers** (the tenants) — home-business owners running cottage-food
  operations. They onboard with no product photos, no copy, no reviews. The
  storefront is their primary commercial surface. The job: signal "this is a
  real, professional business" to first-time customers from day 1, even before
  they've taken a single product photo.

The platform's design must serve both: customers must want to buy, bakers must
look credible without doing photography work.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **warm · refined · intentional**.

- **Warm** — this is food made by hand, often by a neighbor. Cream-forward
  surfaces, soft contrast, never sterile or corporate.
- **Refined** — the platform's job is to make every tenant look more
  professional than their neighbor's hand-rolled WordPress site. Clean
  typography, generous whitespace, restrained palette discipline.
- **Intentional** — every empty state, every placeholder, every fallback
  should look designed, not absent. A baker with zero uploaded photos should
  still feel like the storefront is "their brand," not a template waiting for
  content.

Voice: confident, plain, slightly editorial. Avoid precious vocabulary
("artisan," "small-batch," "lovingly crafted") — let the layout and
typography carry the craft signal. No emoji in primary copy.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference blend**: Pophams Bakery (London, refined-editorial) crossed with
Honeybear Bake Shop (Charlotte, warm-celebrated). Both use:

- Cream / off-white as the primary surface, not as accent
- Tenant brand color (gold by default) for accent only — logos, buttons,
  dividers, badges, monograms
- Generous whitespace that lets photography pop when present and lets clean
  typography carry the page when photography is absent
- Editorial photography treatment: warm natural light, soft focus, mix of
  close-ups and wide shots
- Restrained decorative system (hairline rules, asterisk dividers, badges)
  rather than illustrative noise

**Anti-references** — the storefront should NOT look like:

- Generic Shopify / Wix bakery template (centered hero photo, four-column
  product grid with rounded shadows, "About Us / Menu / Contact" stripe nav)
- Corporate SaaS (purple-blue gradient hero, "Trusted by 10,000+ teams,"
  pill-shaped CTA in primary green)
- Instagram-y minimalist (centered logo on white, single huge product photo,
  "Shop the collection" CTA — feels precious without earning it)
- Dark-mode-first food site (current state — looks dramatic in mockups but
  reads as "broken" when tenants haven't uploaded photos)

**Theme variants** — `modern` / `classic` / `rustic` / `elegant` / `yellow`
are accent-color + font-pairing variants on a **shared cream-forward base**,
not wholesale dark/light palette swaps. The base aesthetic is consistent;
tenants pick the mood.

**Light vs dark**: light (cream surface) is the default and the design target.
Dark sections may exist as deliberate moments (a black-and-cream
testimonial band, a dark hero overlay on a strong photo) but should never be
the default surface for content blocks.

### Design Principles

1. **Empty looks intentional, not absent.** Every fallback (no product photo,
   no reviews, no gallery) must look like a designed state, not a placeholder
   waiting for content. A first-time customer can't tell whether a bakery
   uploaded zero photos or just hasn't filled in this section yet — and they
   shouldn't be able to.

2. **Cream surface, brand-color accent, dark text.** Default content sits on
   cream. The tenant's `brand_color_primary` drives accents (logo, primary
   CTA, dividers, badges, monograms). Body copy is dark warm-neutral (not
   pure black) on cream for warm contrast. Reserve dark surfaces for
   deliberate moments, not page chrome.

3. **Photography sets the ceiling, not the floor.** When tenants upload great
   photos, the design should celebrate them (large frames, generous space,
   minimal chrome). When they don't, the design should hold up on its own
   through typography, spacing, and the brand color — not collapse to "image
   missing" placeholders.

4. **Restrained decoration carries the brand.** Hairline rules, asterisk or
   ornamental dividers, well-set monograms, badges — small designed elements
   used sparingly. Never glassmorphism, never glowing borders, never SaaS
   gradient pills, never gradient text.

5. **WCAG AA minimum, contrast checked at every theme.** Default is AA-compliant.
   Theme overrides must not break contrast. Body text on cream stays at or
   above 7:1 (AAA) where possible since reading menus is a long-form activity.

6. **Don't conflate "warm" with "low contrast."** The current storefront uses
   warm-on-warm tones that visually wash together. Warmth comes from
   palette (cream + gold + warm-brown text), not from low color contrast
   between text and background.
